# Overview
I'd want to perform a literature review of the current state of the industry. This literature review would review current approaches by other projects, identifying their strengths and weaknesses, and helping to clarify where we want the research to focus and what kinds of standards we should adhere to.

Here are a couple examples of literature reviews of unrelated topics:

- https://annas-archive.pm/scidb/10.3389/fbioe.2018.00075/
- https://annas-archive.pm/scidb/10.1016/j.omega.2018.12.001/

I was sent this by a friend. It is an example of a single-author paper. I think this is a great example of what I'm trying to achieve: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/399723211_Speaking_to_Silicon_Thermodynamic_Reservoir_Computing_with_Bitcoin_Mining_ASICs_and_the_Single-Word_Handshake_Protocol 

# Definition of Done
There is no explicit bar of what "complete" looks like. I'd say if it has a similar level of detail and completeness of a true literature review, the task is complete.

More strategically, at the end of the literature review it should give the research some structure and give ideas as what what an early series of prototypes may look like, and what kinds of areas we'd need to cover in a whitepaper.

# Recommendation
I am thinking that our literature review should be read a bit easier than a "true" literature review. I'm thinking it should be somewhat similar to an in-depth blog article or, perhaps, even a wiki of sorts. We could draft (and optionally host) that wiki up here in Obsidian. We could then render the wiki in our website for easier reading.

I suggest approaching this problem in an iterative fashion. I'm thinking if you pick a project one-by-one, and write a blog article about that project, that should help cover the key points for the literature review.
