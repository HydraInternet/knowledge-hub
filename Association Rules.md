# Association Rules

## 1. Name of the Association
The name of the incorporated association is **Hydra Internet** (informally known as HydraNet).

## 2. Objects / Purposes
The objects of the Association are:

1. To research, design, develop, and promote open, permissionless, and decentralised communication networks that reduce reliance on centralised internet infrastructure.
   
2. To support the development of technologies, protocols, and systems that enhance censorship resistance, resilience, and accessibility of digital communications, particularly in circumstances where conventional internet services are unavailable, restricted, or unreliable.
   
3. To advance public knowledge and understanding of decentralised networking, peer-to-peer systems, and related technologies through research, experimentation, education, documentation, and public engagement.
   
4. To foster a community of contributors, researchers, and users who collaborate on the exploration and practical deployment of alternative networking approaches.
   
5. To engage with individuals, communities, research institutions, and other not-for-profit bodies, both within Australia and internationally, where such engagement supports the objects of the Association.
   
6. To do all such other lawful things as are incidental or conducive to the attainment of the above objects.

# 3. Membership

## 3.1 Eligibility

1. Membership of the Association is open to any individual who supports the objects of the Association and agrees to be bound by these rules and any Code of Conduct adopted by the Association.

2. Membership is limited to natural persons. Bodies corporate, partnerships, trusts, and other legal entities are not eligible for membership.

3. Membership is intended to reflect active ideological support for the Association and must be supported by either:
	1. payment of applicable membership fees; or
	2. operational or volunteer contributions to the Association that are assessed by the committee as having a value equal to or greater than the applicable membership fees.

4. The committee must determine, by a supermajority of not less than sixty-six percent (66%) of committee members, the value of any operational or volunteer contributions for the purposes of subrule (3)(b).

5. An applicant must agree to comply with any Code of Conduct adopted by the Association, as in force from time to time.

## 3.2 Application for Membership

1. A person applying for membership must submit a written or electronic application in a form approved by the committee.

2. No nomination or endorsement by an existing member is required.

3. An application for membership is taken to be accepted automatically when:
	1. the applicant satisfies the eligibility requirements under rule 3.1; and
	2. the applicant has either:
	    1. paid any applicable membership fees; or
	    2. completed operational or volunteer contributions that the committee has assessed as meeting or exceeding the applicable membership fees.

4. No additional approval or discretionary acceptance process applies to membership applications, subject to these rules.

## 3.3 Commencement of Membership

1. Membership commences immediately upon satisfaction of the conditions set out in rule 3.2(3).

2. Membership is forward-looking. Operational or volunteer contributions may be assessed retrospectively for the purpose of granting membership but do not confer membership rights prior to acceptance.

3. By applying for and accepting membership, a member is taken to have agreed to be bound by:
	1. these rules; and
	2. any Code of Conduct adopted by the Association.

## 3.4 Bad-Faith Participation

1. A member is taken to have engaged in bad-faith participation if the member acts in a manner that is materially inconsistent with the objects, integrity, or good-faith operation of the Association.

2. A determination of bad-faith participation may be made by:
	1. the Founding Chairperson; or
	2. a resolution of not less than sixty-six percent (66%) of the committee members; or
	3. a resolution supported by not less than ninety percent (90%) of ordinary members eligible to vote.

3. A determination of bad-faith participation must specify the date from which the member is taken to have ceased acting in good faith.

4. Upon such determination, the member’s membership ceases with effect from the specified date.

5. A determination under this rule does not automatically invalidate any votes cast by the member prior to cessation.

## 3.5 Recasting of Votes Following Bad-Faith Determination

1. If not less than twenty percent (20%) of ordinary members eligible to vote request it in writing, the Association must conduct a recast vote in respect of any resolution affected by a member whose membership has ceased under rule 3.4.

2. A recast vote:
	1. must be conducted using the membership register as it stood at the relevant point in time; and
	2. may include only members who were eligible at that time and who remain current members at the time of the recast vote.

3. The outcome of a recast vote replaces the outcome of the original vote for all purposes.

## 3.6 Suspension of Membership

1. A member’s membership may be suspended by:
	1. the Founding Chairperson; or
	2. a resolution of not less than sixty-six percent (66%) of committee members; or
	3. a resolution supported by not less than ninety percent (90%) of ordinary members eligible to vote.

3. A suspension must specify the period of suspension.

4. During a period of suspension:
	1. the member has no membership rights; and
	2. the member is taken not to be a member for all purposes of these rules; and
	3. no membership fees accrue or are payable.

5. At the conclusion of the suspension period, membership resumes unless otherwise determined by resolution.

## 3.7 Expulsion of Members

1. A member may be expelled from the Association only by a resolution supported by not less than ninety percent (90%) of ordinary members eligible to vote.

2. A motion for expulsion may be raised by any member and must comply with the notice and procedural requirements applicable to special motions.

3. A member who is expelled ceases to be a member from the date specified in the resolution.

## 3.8 Appeals and Re-Admission

1. A member who has been suspended or expelled may appeal the decision:
	1. at an annual general meeting; or
	2. at a special general meeting.

2. A member may make at most one appeal in any twelve-month period.

3. A person who has been expelled may reapply for membership as a new applicant.

4. A special resolution of not less than ninety percent (90%) of ordinary members eligible to vote may reinstate a former member’s previous membership, but is not required to do so.

5. Unless reinstated under subrule (4), all rights, seniority, and voting eligibility reset upon re-admission.

## 3.9 Cessation of Membership

1. A person ceases to be a member if:
	1. the person resigns in accordance with rule 3.10;
	2. the person fails to pay any required membership fees when due;
	3. the person is suspended or expelled under these rules; or
	4. the person breaches these rules or the Code of Conduct.

2. Cessation for non-payment of fees takes effect immediately unless waived or extended by:
	1. a resolution of not less than sixty-six percent (66%) of committee members; or
	2. a resolution supported by not less than ninety percent (90%) of ordinary members eligible to vote.

3. Upon cessation, all membership rights immediately terminate.

## 3.10 Resignation

1. A member may resign by giving written or electronic notice to the Association.

2. Resignation takes effect immediately upon receipt of the notice, unless a later time is specified in the notice.

3. A resigning member remains liable for any outstanding fees or obligations incurred prior to resignation.

## 3.11 Freeze of Operations
1. If the committee believes the Association is subject to an attack or serious threat to its integrity, the committee may propose a freeze of operations.

2. A freeze of operations takes effect only if supported by not less than ninety percent (90%) of ordinary members eligible to vote.

3. A freeze may be back-dated to a specified point in time, not exceeding one (1) year prior to the date of the resolution.

4. During a freeze of operations:
	1. no new memberships commence;
	2. no memberships cease for non-payment or other reasons;
	3. the membership register is taken to be fixed at the freeze date;
	4. all members recorded at that date retain voting rights; and
	5. existing financial obligations of the Association continue to be met.

6. A freeze of operations does not affect the validity of actions taken prior to the freeze date unless expressly resolved otherwise.

## 5. Classes of Membership and Fees

### 5.1 Classes of Membership
1. The Association consists of **ordinary members** and any **associate members** provided for under this section.
   
2. An ordinary member has full voting rights at general meetings and any other rights conferred on members by these rules or determined by the committee.
   
3. An associate member has the rights of a member other than full voting rights.
   
4. The Association may, by resolution at a general meeting, create one or more classes of associate membership and determine the rights (if any) attaching to each such class.
   
5. A person may only be an ordinary member or belong to one class of associate membership at any time.
   
6. The number of members of any class is not limited unless otherwise determined by resolution at a general meeting.

### 5.2 Membership Fees
1. The committee may determine: a. an entrance fee (if any); and b. a membership fee (if any), payable by members of each class of membership.
   
2. Fees may differ between ordinary members and associate members, and between classes of associate membership.
   
3. Membership fees are payable in the manner and by the dates determined by the committee.
   
4. A member who fails to pay any required membership fee within a period determined by the committee may cease to be a member.
   
5. The committee may, at its discretion, reinstate a person whose membership has ceased due to non-payment, upon payment of any outstanding fees.

## 6. Management Committee

### 6.1 Committee and Powers
1. The committee members are the persons who, as the management committee of the Association, have the power to manage the affairs of the Association.
   
2. Subject to the Act, these rules, and any resolution passed at a general meeting, the committee may do all things necessary or convenient for the proper management of the affairs of the Association.
   
3. The committee must take all reasonable steps to ensure that the Association complies with the Act and these rules.

### 6.2 Composition of the Committee
1. The committee consists of: a. the **Chairperson**; and b. any other ordinary members elected to the committee in accordance with these rules.
   
2. The Chairperson is the principal office holder of the Association and is ultimately responsible for the governance, administration, and operation of the Association.
   
3. There are no other mandatory or specialised committee roles.
   
4. The Chairperson may appoint or authorise committee members or other persons to perform specific duties or tasks in furtherance of the objects of the Association.
   
5. There is no maximum number of committee members.
   
6. The minimum number of committee members is one, being the Chairperson.

### 6.3 Election and Term of Office
1. 1. Any ordinary member may nominate themselves for election to the committee.
   
2. Committee members must be elected by ordinary members at an annual general meeting.
   
3. The committee may, if it considers it necessary, convene a special general meeting for the purpose of electing committee members.
   
4. A committee member holds office for a maximum term of one (1) year.
   
5. Committee members may submit themselves for re-election at each annual general meeting.

### 6.4 Removal of Committee Members
1. A committee member, other than the Chairperson, may be removed from office if the member: a. fails to actively perform their duties or act in the interests of the Association; or b. is removed by resolution at a general meeting.
   
2. A resolution to remove a committee member under subrule (1)(b) must be passed by a simple majority of ordinary members present and entitled to vote at the meeting.
   
3. A committee member who is the subject of a proposed removal must be given reasonable notice and an opportunity to be heard before any decision is made.

### 6.5 Chairperson and Founding Chairperson

#### 6.5.1 Chairperson
1. The Chairperson is elected from among the committee members in accordance with these rules.
   
2. The Chairperson may be removed from office if: a. all committee members other than the Chairperson unanimously agree to the removal; or b. a resolution is passed at a general meeting by not less than seventy-five percent (75%) of the ordinary members present and entitled to vote.
   
3. At an annual general meeting, the Chairperson is subject to re-election and may be replaced by a simple majority vote of ordinary members present and entitled to vote.
   
4. Upon removal or replacement of the Chairperson, the committee must ensure that a replacement Chairperson is elected in accordance with these rules.

#### 6.5.2 Founding Chairperson
1. Upon incorporation of the Association, the **inaugural Chairperson** of the Association is the **Founding Chairperson**.
   
2. The Founding Chairperson role exists for a maximum of five (5) consecutive annual terms from the date of incorporation of the Association.
   
3. During the term of the Founding Chairperson, the Founding Chairperson exercises all powers and responsibilities of the Chairperson.
   
4. The Founding Chairperson may be removed from office prior to the expiry of the five (5) term period only if a resolution is passed at a general meeting by more than ninety percent (90%) of the ordinary members present and entitled to vote.
   
5. At the conclusion of the fifth annual term, the Founding Chairperson role automatically expires and the office reverts to the standard Chairperson role, subject to election and removal in accordance with these rules.

### 6.6 Committee Meetings and Records
1. Committee meetings may be convened by the Chairperson or by any two committee members.
   
2. A quorum for a committee meeting is present when: a. the Chairperson is present; and b. at least seventy-five percent (75%) of the committee members are present.
   
3. Each committee member present at a meeting has one vote.
   
4. Questions arising at a committee meeting are decided by a majority of votes of committee members present.
   
5. Minutes of committee meetings must be kept.
   
6. Record keeping for committee meetings must be carried out by the Chairperson or by a committee member appointed by the meeting.

## 7. General Meetings of Members

### 7.1 Annual General Meeting (AGM)
1. The Association must hold an annual general meeting once in each calendar year.
   
2. The annual general meeting must be conducted online using electronic means that allow members to participate simultaneously. The specific platform or technology used need not be specified in advance.
   
3. The committee must determine the date and time of the annual general meeting.
   
4. The ordinary business of the annual general meeting is as set out in the model rules under the Associations Incorporation Act 2015 (WA), including but not limited to:
	1. confirmation of the minutes of the previous annual general meeting and any special general meetings held since that meeting;
    2. consideration of the committee’s report on the Association’s activities during the preceding financial year;
    3. consideration of the financial statements or financial report for the preceding financial year, as applicable under the Act;
    4. election of committee members and office holders;
    5. appointment or removal of an auditor or reviewer, if required under the Act; and
    6. confirmation or variation of membership fees, if applicable.

5. Any other business of which notice has been properly given may be conducted at the annual general meeting.

### 7.2 Special General Meetings
1. The committee may convene a special general meeting at any time.
   
2. The committee must convene a special general meeting if at least thirty-three percent (33%) of the ordinary members request that a special general meeting be held.
   
3. A request under subrule (2) must:
    1. be made in writing to the committee;
    2. state the purpose of the meeting and the business to be considered; and
    3. be signed or otherwise authenticated by the members making the request.

4. Except as otherwise provided in these rules, the convening, notice, conduct, and business of special general meetings must follow the model rules under the Act.

### 7.3 Notice and Procedure
#### 7.3.1 Notice of General Meetings
1. At least twenty-eight (28) days’ notice must be given to all members for:
    1. an annual general meeting; or
    2. a special general meeting.

2. Notice may be given by electronic means to the contact details recorded in the register of members.
   
3. The notice must specify:
    1. the date and time of the meeting;
    2. that the meeting will be conducted online;
    3. the general nature of the business to be considered; and
    4. any other information required by the Act or these rules.
#### 7.3.2 Quorum
1. Subject to subrules (2) to (7), the quorum for a general meeting is the presence of the Chairperson.
   
2. Any member may elect, by giving written notice to the committee at least seven (7) days before the meeting, to require their attendance at a particular general meeting.
   
3. If one or more members have elected to attend a meeting under subrule (2), the quorum for that meeting is:
    1. the presence of the Chairperson; and
    2. the attendance of at least sixty-six percent (66%) of the members who elected to attend.

4. The committee must make reasonable efforts to accommodate members who have elected to attend a meeting, including by selecting a mutually convenient meeting time where practicable.

5. A member who has elected to attend a meeting but is unable to do so must give at least twenty-four (24) hours’ notice to the committee. If such notice is given, the member is not taken to be absent for the purposes of these rules.

6. If a member elects to attend a meeting and fails to attend without giving notice under subrule (5), the member is taken to be absent.

7. Where a member is absent under subrule (6), the committee may revoke that member’s membership if:
    1. the Founding Chairperson agrees to the revocation; or
    2. at least eighty percent (80%) of the committee members agree to the revocation.
#### 7.3.2 Voting, Proxies, and Participation
1. Voting entitlements, voting procedures, and the requirements for passing resolutions at general meetings are as set out in the model rules under the Act.
2. Proxy appointments are permitted in accordance with the model rules under the Act.
3. Members may participate in a general meeting by electronic means that allow simultaneous communication, and a member participating in this way is taken to be present at the meeting.
## 8. Notices and Voting
### 8.1 Notices to Members
1. Any notice or other document required to be given to a member under these rules must be given in writing.

2. Notices may be given to a member by:
    1. electronic transmission to an email address or other electronic contact recorded in the register of members; or
    2. any other method permitted under the model rules or the Act.

3. A notice is taken to have been given:
    1. if sent electronically, at the time it is transmitted, unless the sender is notified that delivery has failed; or
    2. if given by another permitted method, in accordance with the model rules.

4. All members, regardless of class of membership, are entitled to receive notice of, and to attend, any general meeting of the Association, subject to these rules.

### 8.2 Voting Entitlements
1. Voting rights at general meetings are as set out in the model rules under the Associations Incorporation Act 2015 (WA).

2. Each ordinary member has one vote on any question arising at a general meeting.

3. Associate members do not have voting rights unless expressly provided for by these rules or by resolution at a general meeting.

4. A member is only entitled to vote if:
    1. the member was a member entitled to vote at the time notice of the meeting was given; and
	2. any fees or other monies payable by the member to the Association have been paid.

### 8.3 Method of Voting
1. Subject to these rules and the Act, voting at a general meeting may be conducted:
    1. by show of hands;
    2. by electronic means; or
    3. by any other method permitted under the model rules.

2. Except where a special resolution is required, a motion is carried if a simple majority of the votes cast by ordinary members present or represented is in favour of the motion.

3. If the votes on a question are equal, the Chairperson has a second or casting vote, in accordance with the model rules.

### 8.4 Proxies
1. An ordinary member may appoint another ordinary member as their proxy to vote and speak on their behalf at a general meeting, in accordance with the model rules.

2. The appointment of a proxy must:
    1. be in writing; and
    2. be received by the Association in accordance with the timeframes set out in the model rules.

3. A proxy appointment received electronically is valid.

4. Proxy participation does not affect quorum unless otherwise provided for under these rules.

### 8.5 Electronic Participation

1. A general meeting may be conducted using electronic means that allow members to participate simultaneously.

2. A member who participates in a meeting by electronic means is taken to be present at the meeting and, if the member votes, to have voted in person.

## 9. Requisition of General Meetings

1. Ordinary members of the Association may require that a special general meeting be convened.

2. A requirement to convene a special general meeting must be made by not less than thirty-three percent (33%) of the ordinary members of the Association.

3. A requirement under subrule (2) must:
    1. be made in writing to the committee;
    2. state the purpose of the proposed meeting and the business to be considered; and
    3. be signed or otherwise authenticated by the members making the requirement.
    
4. Upon receiving a valid requirement under this rule, the committee must convene a special general meeting in accordance with these rules and the model rules under the Associations Incorporation Act 2015 (WA).

5. Except as expressly provided in this section, the procedure, notice requirements, timing, and conduct of a special general meeting requisitioned by members are to follow the model rules under the Act.

## 10. Control and Use of Funds
1. The funds of the Association must be applied solely towards the promotion of the objects and purposes of the Association and must not be distributed directly or indirectly to any member, except as permitted by the Associations Incorporation Act 2015 (WA) and these rules.

2. The Association must open and maintain one or more accounts in the name of the Association with a financial institution approved by the committee.

3. All money received by or on behalf of the Association must be deposited into an Association account as soon as practicable after receipt.

4. Subject to any restriction imposed by a resolution of a general meeting, the committee may approve expenditure on behalf of the Association.

5. The committee may authorise expenditure without prior committee approval as follows:
    1. any committee member may incur expenditure on behalf of the Association up to a maximum amount of **two thousand Australian dollars (AUD $2,000)** per item or transaction;
    2. the Chairperson may incur expenditure on behalf of the Association up to a maximum amount of **ten thousand Australian dollars (AUD $10,000)** per item or transaction.

6. Any expenditure exceeding the limits set out in subrule (5) must be approved by a resolution of the committee before the expenditure is incurred.

7. The committee may determine that any proposed expenditure, regardless of amount, is significant or exceptional and require that it be approved by resolution at a general meeting.

8. A payment may be made to a committee member out of the funds of the Association only if:
    1. the payment is in good faith as reimbursement for reasonable expenses properly incurred on behalf of the Association, or for services or goods provided to the Association in the ordinary course of business; and
    2. the payment is authorised by a resolution of the Association, in accordance with the Act.

9. The committee must ensure that appropriate records are kept of all income and expenditure of the Association in accordance with the Act.

## 11. Financial Year
1. The financial year of the Association is the period of twelve (12) months commencing on **1 July** in each year and ending on **30 June** of the following year.

2. The first financial year of the Association commences on the date of incorporation and ends on the first occurring 30 June after that date.

## 12. Accounts and Records

1. The Association must keep financial records that:
   a. correctly record and explain its transactions and financial position and performance; and  
   b. enable true and fair financial statements or financial reports to be prepared in accordance with the Associations Incorporation Act 2015 (WA).

2. The committee must ensure that the Association complies with all requirements of Part 5 of the Act relating to financial records, financial statements, and financial reports.

3. Financial records must be retained for at least seven (7) years after the completion of the transactions to which they relate.

4. The treasurer is responsible for the custody and control of the Association’s financial records, financial statements, and financial reports, unless the committee determines otherwise.

5. Other books and records of the Association must be kept in the custody or under the control of the secretary, unless the committee determines otherwise.

## ## 13. Inspection of Records

1. A member is entitled to inspect, free of charge:
   a. the register of members;  
   b. the record of committee members and other authorised persons; and  
   c. any other record or document of the Association,  
   in accordance with the Associations Incorporation Act 2015 (WA).

2. A member wishing to inspect a record or document must contact the secretary to make the necessary arrangements.

3. Inspection must be conducted at a reasonable time and place determined by the committee.

4. A member may make a copy of, or take an extract from, a record or document inspected under this rule but must not remove the original record or document.

5. A member must not use or disclose information obtained from an inspection except for:
   a. a purpose directly connected with the affairs of the Association; or  
   b. complying with a requirement of the Act.

## ## 14. Dispute Resolution

### 14.1 Application

1. This rule applies to disputes:
	1. between members; or  
	2. between one or more members and the Association.

### 14.2 Resolution Process

1. The parties to a dispute must attempt to resolve the dispute between themselves within fourteen (14) days after the dispute has come to the attention of each party.

2. If the dispute is not resolved under subrule (1), any party may give written notice to the secretary setting out:
	1. the parties to the dispute; and  
	2. the matters the subject of the dispute.

4. Within twenty-eight (28) days after receiving the notice, the committee must convene a committee meeting to consider and determine the dispute.

5. Each party to the dispute must be given at least seven (7) days’ notice of the committee meeting and a reasonable opportunity to make written or oral submissions.

6. The committee must give each party written notice of its determination and the reasons for the determination within seven (7) days after the meeting.

### 14.3 Mediation

1. A party to the dispute may, within fourteen (14) days after receiving notice of the committee’s determination, request the appointment of a mediator.

2. If mediation is requested, a mediator must be appointed in accordance with the model rules under the Act.

3. The mediator must not determine the dispute but must assist the parties to attempt to resolve it in good faith.

4. Mediation must be conducted confidentially.

5. The costs of mediation are to be borne by the party or parties requesting the mediation.

## 15. Alteration of Rules

### 15.1 Alteration by Special Resolution

1. Subject to subrule 15.2, the Association may alter, rescind, or add to these rules only by special resolution passed at a general meeting of the Association, in accordance with the Associations Incorporation Act 2015 (WA).

2. Any alteration, rescission, or addition to these rules under subrule (1) must comply with:
	1. Part 3 Division 2 of the Act; and
	2. any requirement to obtain approval from, or to lodge documents with, the Commissioner.

4. An alteration to these rules does not take effect until all requirements of the Act relating to the alteration have been satisfied.

### 15.2 Founding Chairperson Power to Amend Rules

1. Notwithstanding subrule 15.1, during the term of the Founding Chairperson as defined in rule 6.5.2, the Founding Chairperson may alter, rescind, or add to these rules at any time by written instrument.

2. An alteration made under subrule (1):
	1. takes effect on the date specified in the written instrument, subject to compliance with the Act; and
	2. has the same force and effect as if it had been made by special resolution of the Association.

4. The Founding Chairperson must ensure that any alteration made under this rule:
	1. complies with the Act; and
	2. is lodged with, and approved by, the Commissioner where required under sections 31 or 33 of the Act.

6. As soon as practicable after making an alteration under this rule, the Founding Chairperson must:
	1. provide notice of the alteration to all members; and
	2. ensure that an updated copy of the rules is made available to members.

### 15.3 Expiry of Founding Chairperson Powers

1. Upon the expiry of the Founding Chairperson role under rule 6.5.2, subrule 15.2 ceases to have effect.

2. From that time, all alterations, rescissions, or additions to these rules must be made strictly in accordance with subrule 15.1.

## 16. Common Seal

1. The Association may have a common seal.

2. If the Association has a common seal:
	1. the name of the Association must appear in legible characters on the seal;  
	2. the seal must be kept in the custody of the secretary or another committee member authorised by the committee; and  
	3. the seal may only be used by authority of the committee.

4. The use of the common seal must be witnessed by:
	1. two committee members; or  
	2. one committee member and a person authorised by the committee.

6. The secretary must keep a written record of each use of the common seal.

## 17. Custody of Books and Securities

1. Subject to subrule (2), the books and any securities of the Association must be kept in the custody or under the control of the secretary.

2. The financial records and, as applicable, the financial statements or financial reports of the Association must be kept in the custody or under the control of the treasurer.

3. Subrules (1) and (2) apply except as otherwise determined by resolution of the committee.

4. The books of the Association must be retained for at least seven (7) years.

## 18. Distribution of Surplus Property

1. In this rule, surplus property means property remaining after satisfaction of:
   a. the debts and liabilities of the Association; and  
   b. the costs, charges, and expenses of winding up or cancellation of incorporation.

2. Upon the winding up of the Association or the cancellation of its incorporation, the surplus property must not be distributed to any member.

3. The surplus property must be distributed in accordance with a special resolution of the Association to one or more of the following:
	1. an incorporated association;  
	2. a company limited by guarantee registered under the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth);  
	3. an organisation holding a current licence under the Charitable Collections Act 1946 (WA);  
	4. an organisation whose rules prohibit the distribution of property to its members; or  
	5. a non-distributing co-operative registered under the Co-operatives Act 2009 (WA).

7. This rule is subject to section 24 of the Associations Incorporation Act 2015 (WA).

## 19. Winding Up

1. The Association may be wound up voluntarily only by special resolution in accordance with the Associations Incorporation Act 2015 (WA).

2. The winding up of the Association must be carried out in accordance with Part 9 of the Act.

3. Upon winding up, any surplus property must be dealt with in accordance with rule 18.
